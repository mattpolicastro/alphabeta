What do my results say?

Reads a finished test: whether each variant beat the control, by how much, and how sure you can be. Conversion rates get a two-proportion z-test; means get Welch's t-test. Up to three treatments against one control, Bonferroni-corrected.

One-tailed means you declared treatment beats control before looking; a worse treatment can only read as inconclusive.

Family-wise. With three or four arms each comparison runs at α ÷ (arms − 1); intervals are at the same level.

Numbers are written in their shortest exact decimal form (0.05, not 0.050); lists use a literal comma. Binomial: two-proportion z-test with pooled variance under H₀ and no continuity correction — R prop.test(correct = FALSE), statsmodels proportions_ztest; the interval on the difference is Wald with each arm's own variance, which is what prop.test reports. Continuous: Welch's t-test with Welch–Satterthwaite degrees of freedom — R t.test(var.equal = FALSE), scipy ttest_ind_from_stats(equal_var = False). Relative lift intervals are the delta method on treatment / control − 1 with the same quantile. More than two arms: each treatment against the control at α ÷ (arms − 1), Bonferroni, the same split the sample-size tool sizes for. Departures from the reference tool on forwarddigital.org: it uses the unpooled z for the p-value, a pooled-variance z (not Welch) for means, divides the difference interval by the control rate for relative lift, and takes the one-tailed direction from the data.
